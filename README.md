# Credit-Risk-Analysis

**Analysed 11,162 Portuguese Bank Data**

# Business Problem
**The bank wants to understand which customer segments are more suitable for lending and which are more likely to experience negative account balances. By analysing customer demographics and financial characteristics, including job type, education level, account balance, existing loans and mortgage status, the bank aims to develop customer risk profiles to support more informed lending decisions and minimise potential credit risk.**

# Business Questions

**1. Who is most likely to go into overdraft ?
2. What are the characteristics (age, education, occupation and account balance) of customers who take personal loans?
3. Which customer segments demonstrate potentially higher financial risk?
4. Is educational attainment associated with financial behaviour (account balances, personal loans, mortgage uptake and overdraft)?**

# FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 

**Customer Segmentation 
Management Employees (White-Collar) & Self-Employed: Generally customer with stronger educational profiles have healthier account balances, with relatively low negative-balance rates. White Collar customers have a 4% negative-balance rate, suggesting a potentially lower-risk customer segment.
Retired: Highest proportion of above-average bank balances (42.5%), suggesting stronger accumulated savings, alongside low personal loan uptake (7%) and negative balances (3%).
Blue-Collar, Services & Entrepreneurs: Higher borrowing levels and negative-balance rates, particularly among Blue-Collar customers (11% negative balances). These groups require closer affordability and debt assessments, most fall between the age range 30-50.
Housemaids: Despite lower educational attainment and occupational status, only 3% have negative balances and personal loan uptake is 7.3%. This demonstrates that education and occupation alone do not determine financial behaviour.**
    
# Reccommendations: 
**Occupation and education help predict customer financial profiles, but borrowing behaviour and account balances provide a clearer picture of their financial position. Customer assessment officers should consider all these factors rather than relying on education or occupation alone.**
