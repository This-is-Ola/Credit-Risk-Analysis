# Credit-Risk-Analysis
Bank Credit Risk Analysis, who goes Into overdraft, Indicators of a pottenitial high risk customers.
Analysed 11,162 bank customers to identify job and education as predictors of loan and overdraft risk. Source: Bank Marketing data, originally for term deposit campaign, repurposed here for credit risk.
Business Problem- The bank wanted to know who is safe to lend to, and who is likely to go into negative balance. I used job, education, balance, loan and housing to build a risk profile.
Business Question - 
What kind of customer gets a personal loan? What is their age, education, job, balance? And who ends up with a negative balance?
Is a customer's occupation associated with their financial behaviour, particularly account balances, personal loan uptake, Mortgage uptake and negative account balances?
FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
Customer Segmentation 
Management Employees (White-Collar) & Self-Employed: Generally customer with stronger educational profiles have healthier account balances, with relatively low negative-balance rates. White Collar customers have a 4% negative-balance rate, suggesting a potentially lower-risk customer segment.
Retired: Highest proportion of above-average bank balances (42.5%), suggesting stronger accumulated savings, alongside low personal loan uptake (7%) and negative balances (3%).
Blue-Collar, Services & Entrepreneurs: Higher borrowing levels and negative-balance rates, particularly among Blue-Collar customers (11% negative balances). These groups require closer affordability and debt assessments, most fall between the age range 30-50.
Housemaids: Despite lower educational attainment and occupational status, only 3% have negative balances and personal loan uptake is 7.3%. This demonstrates that education and occupation alone do not determine financial behaviour.
    
Reccommendations: Occupation and education help predict customer financial profiles, but borrowing behaviour and account balances provide a clearer picture of their financial position. Customer assessment officers should consider all these factors rather than relying on education or occupation alone.
